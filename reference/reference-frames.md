# Reference Frames — how to build the yardstick (never a canned one)

The absent-hub scan in `gap-heuristics.md` needs something to measure absence
against, or "they are missing X" is only an opinion, and worse, it may be the
cartographer's own bias smuggled in as fact. This file is **not a checklist**. It
is how to build the yardstick fresh for each territory, from outside evidence,
never from the agent's head or the user's offhand remarks.

## The rule: the frame is earned, never assumed

Build the frame per run, from two sources, and cite every element to one of them:

1. **The user's own frameworks.** Ask the user directly: *what models, canvases,
   standards or frameworks do you use for this kind of work?* A platform-design
   toolkit, a domain SOP, an ISO or safety standard, a governance model. These are
   legitimate lenses because they are explicit, named and established, not invented
   on the spot.

2. **At least two deeply-researched analogues.** Comparable bodies of work in the
   same shape and scale as the territory. For each one, research in depth:
   - what operating model did they actually use?
   - what is documented about their successes **and** their failures?
   - what dimensions did they have to carry to work (governance, financing,
     security, lifecycle, and so on)?
   Naming a reference ("CERN does X") without studying how it works and where it
   broke is the shallow move the cartographer refuses. An analogue earns its place
   by depth, not by being cited.

## Match the shape and scale, not a fixed list

"Ecosystem" is scale-free. A small business of interacting people and software is
an ecosystem; a cross-border megaproject is an ecosystem. Pick analogues that match
**this** territory's shape and scale. A corner shop is measured against other small
operations, not against CERN. Never import a megaproject frame onto a small domain,
or the reverse.

## Derive the dimensions, do not assert them

The dimensions a complete model carries are **read off** the researched analogues
and the user's frameworks, each with a source. If two comparable cases both carried
a lifecycle plan and this territory shows none, that absence is grounded in
evidence. A dimension with no source behind it is a bias. Drop it.

## Worked instance (Einstein Telescope)

For the ET territory the frame would be built from at least:

- **Virgo**, the existing reference detector: how it was built and operated, what
  worked, what was hard. Studied, not cited.
- **CERN or an ERIC-style multinational research infrastructure**: its governance
  and financing model, and its documented frictions.
- **The EU framework for multi-country research collaborations**: how shared
  responsibility, funding and IP are handled in practice.
- Plus whatever frameworks the technology manager brings to the work.

From researching those, the dimensions to check the ET map against emerge *with
evidence*: governance form, a cross-border financing vehicle, security (physical
and cyber), lifecycle and decommissioning, cross-border IP, value capture. These
are the **output** of researching ET's analogues, not a universal template. A
different territory yields a different frame.

## What this replaces

An earlier version of this file listed those dimensions directly, as if they were
the domain's truth. They were one person's intuitions from a conversation. That
muddies the map with the cartographer's bias. The frame must be earned from
evidence every time. If you cannot cite where a dimension came from, it does not go
in the frame.
