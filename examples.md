## CARD — Site-Selection Working Group  ·  [Actor · governance body]

- **Installed by:** the Einstein Telescope Board of Governmental Representatives (BGR)
- **Led by:** Andrew Harrison
- **Composition:** 20 core + 8 additional; balanced country representation; **4
  experts deliberately from non-host countries** (a built-in neutrality device).
- **Four sub-groups:** (1) scientific/technical requirements, (2) civil works /
  environment / geology, (3) contextual factors (ecosystem, community,
  connectivity), (4) overall costings and finance.
- **Output:** *not* a site assessment. A set of criteria + weighting recommended
  into the candidate **bidbooks**, so evidence is comparable across sites. Report
  due end-2026.
- **Status:** **live** (source: einsteintelescope-emr.eu, Harrison interview)
- **Backlinks:** installed-by → BGR · coordinates-with → ETO (geometry, separate
  track) · governs → the bidbook Instrument · feeds → the site Decision (pending) ·
  touches → all three candidate Jurisdictions (Sardinia, EMR, Lusatia).
- **Hits:** the bidbook criteria; what each site must evidence; data comparability.
- **Does not hit:** the geometry decision (run separately by ETO, on purpose). The
  wrong neighbour is "the site group decides the shape too." It does not.

- **Jurisdiction:** EU (EFRE) via the EMR programme (D/NL/B)
- **Parties:** consortia from at least 2 of the 3 countries — cross-border is
  **mandatory**. That mandate is the point.
- **Money-routing:** **YES** — the one channel that moves money across the border.

  line closes, the only border-crossing money channel for applied work closes.
- **Does not hit:** fundamental research funding and the ~1.9 Mrd build. Wrong
  neighbour: "Interreg is the EU money, so it funds the telescope." No.

## GHOST — Cross-border construction financing vehicle  ·  [Instrument / Jurisdiction · GHOST]

- **What everyone references:** the ~1.9 to 2.4 Mrd build, "funded across DE/BE/NL."
- **Why still a ghost:** the BGR, the Working Group and ETO are live (governance
  *procedure* wired), and sub-group 4 studies "overall costings and finance." But
  studying costs is not a financing vehicle. No legal/financial entity yet receives
  and routes construction money across the three states. NL frames it as national
  interest; Flanders and Wallonia hold *reserved* contributions separately. No
  source routes cross-border infrastructure money.
- **Tripwire:** "the BGR exists, so the funding is handled." A governance procedure
  is not a financing vehicle. Building a bid plan on that assumption designs a world
  that does not exist.
- **Status:** **ghost** (absent from every verified source → not a fact).

## TENSION — Siloed national funding vs one integrated ET

Both sides live, pulling now:

- Research money is jurisdiction-locked (DFG only DE, NWO only NL, FWO only
  Flanders, FNRS only frankophone BE; ERC single-researcher). Business engagement
  is nationally brokered too (LIOF/NMWP/FWO/ULiège). The border dissolves only at
  the Interreg/SME overlay and the CRAFT registry.
- The ET needs one integrated cross-border program and one financing vehicle.
- **Mechanism:** each euro is locked to its national funder's territory; a truly
  joint program has no home funder, and no funder may pay across the border.
- **Survives solving everything else?** Yes — it is structural to how European
  public funding is organised. That is what makes it a tension, not a problem.

**Related gradient:** *Moving toward a single host-nation framing (NL national
interest + reserved Flemish/Walloon contributions), at the cost of the shared
financing vehicle that never gets built because the national rails already work for
each country's own institutions. Threshold: the site decision, after which the host
framing hardens.*
