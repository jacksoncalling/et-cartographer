# North Star — Stone Masters

## The structural finding

The busiest node in this map is [[Judgment Stock]], and it is a **ghost**. Every
operational decision (dispatch, sick cover, job acceptance, client relationships,
crew management) routes through the same two people's heads, but that knowledge
has no external representation, no holder besides Bram and Niels themselves, and
no instrument that makes it legible to anyone else.

The owner's stated destination (step out of daily ops) and his stated blind side
(why do jobs run smooth vs. messy) are the same gap seen from two directions:
the judgment that runs the business is unwired.

## The gap, plainly

Two live clusters exist:
1. **The operations cluster** (crews, dispatch, equipment, PlanCraft, on-site execution)
2. **The governance cluster** (Bram + Niels's decisions, job acceptance, sick cover)

They are connected only by the two people. There is no **instrument** (a rubric,
a protocol, a visible record) bridging them. If those two people step back,
the connection between "what should happen" and "what does happen" disappears.

## What this map does NOT say

It does not say "build a tool" or "hire a manager" or "write a playbook." It
says: the connecting tissue between decision and execution is two human memories,
and that tissue has a name (Bram calls it his "blind side") but no wiring.
What to do with that finding is a separate question.

## Reference frame status

**Thin.** No researched analogues yet. Comparable cases (trade firms that
successfully externalized owner-judgment: a scaffolding company, a specialized
contractor) have not been studied. The gap above is grounded in Bram's own
words, not in evidence from comparable operations. To upgrade this map:
research two comparable firms that made this transition, study what they
actually did (not just that they did it), and derive what dimensions mattered.
